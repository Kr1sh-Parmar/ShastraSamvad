What connects to what

The college GPU computer is the brain. The Jetson Orin Nano is the kiosk with the screen, touch, microphone and speaker. The 7" Waveshare LCD is the Jetson’s display.

Confirm the exact Jetson model from the label on the board. This page assumes an Orin Nano Developer Kit, which outputs video on DisplayPort, not HDMI (so the HDMI LCD needs a DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapter) and runs from the kit’s own power supply, not a 5V/4A adapter. Check your board’s datasheet before powering it.

Bring-up order

  1. Computer alone. Backend and Next.js page on the college computer. Nothing else until this works.
  2. Jetson and LCD alone. Picture at the right size, touch moves the pointer.
  3. Audio on the Jetson. Record and play a clip through the codec and speaker.
  4. Network. If the college network isolates devices, use a direct Ethernet cable with fixed addresses, for example 192.168.50.1 and 192.168.50.2. Ping the computer from the Jetson.
  5. Open the backend. Start it with --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000 and allow inbound TCP 8000 in the firewall.
  6. Point the kiosk at the computer. Set BACKEND_HTTP on line 135 of frontend/kiosk.html to the computer’s address, and add the page’s origin to CORS_ORIGINS in backend/config.py.
  7. Check. Open http://<computer-ip>:8000/health from the Jetson. All true except vad.
  8. Run. chromium-browser --kiosk frontend/kiosk.html. Typed question first, then spoken.

What this test proves

Proves: screen, touch, microphone, speaker, network and the whole spoken question-and-answer loop work together.

Does not prove: anything about the Jetson running the AI. It loads no model here, so the llama-cpp-python, whisper.cpp and Silero VAD paths stay untested, and the Jetson’s own latency and memory are still unknown. That is the next stage, with everything on the Jetson, measured and labeled separately.
